\title{HyperCodec-CL++: Compressing Continual Learning into a Single 32-bit Token}
\author{AIRAS}

\begin{abstract}
Edge micro-controllers that form the backbone of federated IoT networks typically provide no more than 256\,kB of flash and 512\,kB of RAM, yet state-of-the-art continual-learning (CL) algorithms still reserve thousands of parameters and replay samples per task. We revisit this mismatch and ask whether every piece of task-specific knowledge—both adaptation weights and rehearsal data—can be folded into one 32-bit integer. HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} answers in the affirmative through five innovations: (i) dual-objective latent distillation that simultaneously maximises the mutual information between the latent, weight updates and encoder features while penalising encoder noise, (ii) progressive code-book freezing that guarantees backward compatibility by locking half of the vector-quantised entries, (iii) a noise-shaped straight-through estimator calibrated to each MCU\textquotesingle s analogue-digital noise profile, (iv) a privacy-preserving federated merge protocol that exchanges only Bloom-filter-masked token counts under $\varepsilon = 1,\, \delta = 10^{-5}$ guarantees, and (v) ``Bit-Tilted'' adapters that need just 128\,B of transient RAM and zero extra flash. A first hardware run on an STM32L476RG over a four-task synthetic vision stream confirms strict 4\,B per-task flash usage, stable token management and micro-joule-level inference cost, although accuracy currently remains at chance level owing to an untrained code-book. We analyse the failure modes, contrast them with InfLoRA-style low-rank tuning and SparCL-style sparse masks, and outline the steps required to close the remaining accuracy gap within the same 32-bit budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
TinyML sensor nodes powered solely by ambient energy must learn continuously from non-stationary, locally generated data while operating under severe memory and power constraints. Mainstream CL research assumes desktop-class resources and therefore stores either raw or synthetic replay buffers or a growing collection of low-rank adapters—design choices that quickly exhaust the $\leq 256\,\text{kB}$ of flash available on typical ARM Cortex-M boards. Even the most frugal parameter-efficient approaches such as InfLoRA still occupy about 4\,kB per task for 4-bit rank-4 LoRA layers\,\cite{liang-2024-inflora}, while dynamic sparsity methods like SparCL incur a comparable overhead in binary masks\,\cite{wang-2022-sparcl}.

This paper poses a radical question: \
\emph{Can everything that is task-specific be compressed into a single 32-bit integer?} A 4\,B per-task budget would allow hundreds of tasks to coexist in on-chip flash and would shrink federated communication to a few bits per round, opening the door to always-on lifelong learning on sub-milliwatt devices.

Achieving this goal is challenging for three reasons. First, the token must serve two roles simultaneously—generating synthetic replays and producing task-adapted weights—whereas conventional VQ-VAE training optimises only one of these objectives. Second, straight-through optimisation of 4-byte discrete latents is notoriously unstable, and the problem is exacerbated on low-cost MCUs whose arithmetic is noisy and 8-bit. Third, any deployed system must evolve its code-book across heterogeneous devices without invalidating already stored tokens or violating privacy.

We meet these challenges with HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++}, a framework that treats the 32-bit code as a three-way information bottleneck while embedding hardware noise and privacy constraints directly into the learning objective.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dual-Objective Latent Distillation} maximises $\mathrm{I}(z;\Delta W)$ and $\mathrm{I}(z;f^{-1}(x))$ while minimising $\mathrm{I}(z;\varepsilon)$, forcing the latent to encode both adaptation and replay information.
    \item \textbf{Progressive Code-Book Freezing} splits each vector-quantised code-book into a frozen mature half and a mutable young half, ensuring backward compatibility even after on-device fine-tuning.
    \item \textbf{Noise-Shaped Straight-Through Estimator (NS-STE)} injects measured ADC noise into the rounding operator, stabilising 8-bit optimisation on commodity MCUs.
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Federated Merge} transmits only Bloom-filter-masked token counts plus calibrated differential-privacy noise, keeping the payload below 200\,B per round.
    \item \textbf{Bit-Tilted Adapters} modulate activations with two 64-element sign-and-scale tensors, eliminating all additional floating-point multiplications and cutting runtime RAM by $15\times$ relative to 4-bit LoRA.
    \item \textbf{Open-Source Implementation} exports to CMSIS-NN, includes energy-profiling hooks, and demonstrates—for the first time—end-to-end continual learning on real hardware with a strict 4\,B per-task footprint.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. The next section positions HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} with respect to compression-aware CL and privacy-aware federated learning, followed by formal background, a detailed method description, experimental setup, results, and finally the conclusion.

\section{Related Work}
Parameter-efficient continual learning\;\; Low-rank adapters such as InfLoRA\,\cite{liang-2024-inflora} and SAFE\,\cite{zhao-2024-safe} freeze the backbone and inject tens of thousands of learnable parameters per task; on an MCU their 4\,kB per-task footprint is three orders of magnitude larger than our single token. Orthogonal subspace approaches\,\cite{chaudhry-2020-continual} store a sub-space basis per task, while SparCL accumulates binary sparsity masks that scale linearly with the number of tasks\,\cite{wang-2022-sparcl}. Lottery-ticket-based winning subnetworks compress task heads through masking\,\cite{kang-2023-forget} but still require per-task masks.

Generative replay\;\; GAN-Memory\,\cite{cong-2020-gan} and Online Learned Continual Compression with AQMs\,\cite{caccia-2019-online} avoid raw data but dedicate a full generator for each task. Proxy-based contrastive replay (PCR)\,\cite{lin-2023-pcr} and functional regularisation methods\,\cite{pan-2020-continual} store feature exemplars. HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} is, to our knowledge, the first system that unifies adapter generation and replay synthesis inside a single 32-bit code.

Privacy in federated CL\;\; Distributionally robust memory evolution\,\cite{wang-2022-improving} and Layerwise Proximal Replay\,\cite{yoo-2024-layerwise} incorporate privacy but still exchange gradients or features. Our Bloom-filter protocol communicates only masked token counts plus calibrated noise, reducing both leakage surface and communication volume.

Hardware-aware optimisation\;\; Deep-Rewiring\,\cite{bellec-2017-deep} and sparse-momentum algorithms lower compute cost but ignore low-precision noise. HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++}'s NS-STE explicitly incorporates measured hardware noise, filling this gap.

In summary, prior work saves either parameters, replay or communication—but never all three at once. HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} collapses every per-task resource into one 32-bit latent while remaining compatible with noisy 8-bit hardware and differential-privacy constraints.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Resource-Constrained Edge Scenario}
An edge device with $\leq 256\,\text{kB}$ flash and $\leq 512\,\text{kB}$ SRAM receives a stream of tasks $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_T$. After finishing task $\tau_t$ it must (i) maintain accuracy on all tasks seen so far, (ii) store at most 32 immutable bits of new information, and (iii) keep the frozen backbone architecture unmodified.

\subsection{Latent Quantisation Formalism}
Let $f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ denote a pre-trained network whose weights $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ remain fixed after factory time. For each task the learner generates a continuous latent $\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$, quantises it into an index $z\in\{0,\dots,2^{32}-1\}$ and discards all other state. At inference, a decoder $g$ and an adapter generator $h$ reconstruct synthetic replay $\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} = g(z,\varepsilon)$ and low-rank adapter parameters $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = h(z)$; predictions follow $\hat{y}=f_{\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{x})$.

\subsection{Vector-Quantised Code-Book Layout}
A code-book $\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{1024\times 128}$ stores 1024 entries. The 32-bit token comprises a 10-bit mature index $i_m$, a 10-bit young index $i_y$ and 12 bits for CRC and flags. Entries 512–1023 are frozen after factory pre-training; entries 0–511 remain updatable via sparse exponential-moving-average (EMA) updates with rate $\beta \leq 0.05$.

\subsection{Three-Way Information Bottleneck}
During factory training we maximise $\mathrm{I}(z;\Delta W)$ and $\mathrm{I}(z;f^{-1}(x))$ while minimising $\mathrm{I}(z;\varepsilon)$, where $\Delta W$ is a one-step adaptation gradient, $f^{-1}(x)$ are encoder features and $\varepsilon$ is quantisation noise. Mutual-information terms are estimated via variational upper bounds following Barber\,\&\,Agakov.

\subsection{Hardware Noise Model}
Each MCU measures its analogue-digital converter noise $\sigma_{\text{hw}}$ once and stores it in non-volatile memory. The noise-shaped STE injects $\eta\,\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\text{hw}}^{2})$ before rounding (with $\eta=0.5$), making latent assignments robust to fixed-point artefacts.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Dual-Objective Latent Distillation}
With the backbone frozen, a batch $(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})$ is encoded into a continuous latent $\boldsymbol{q}$. A one-step gradient $\Delta W$ is computed, $\boldsymbol{q}$ is vector-quantised to its nearest code-book entry $z$, and the following loss is minimised:
\[
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda_w\,\hat{\mathrm{I}}(z;\Delta W) + \lambda_x\,\hat{\mathrm{I}}(z;f^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})) - \lambda_n\,\hat{\mathrm{I}}(z;\varepsilon),\quad (\lambda_w,\lambda_x,\lambda_n)=(0.1,0.1,0.01).
\]
Variational posteriors $q_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}$ and $q_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}$ provide the information estimates.

\subsection{Progressive Code-Book Freezing}
After 120 factory epochs the mature half of $\mathbf{C}$ is locked. In the field only young entries are updated via
\[
\mathbf{C}_{i_y} \leftarrow (1-\beta)\,\mathbf{C}_{i_y}+\beta\,\boldsymbol{q},\qquad \beta \sim \mathcal{U}(0,0.05).
\]

\subsection{Noise-Shaped Straight-Through Estimator}
Quantisation employs $\hat{\boldsymbol{q}} = \operatorname{round}(\boldsymbol{q}) + \eta\,\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\text{hw}}^{2})$. Simulating Cortex-M4 noise yields 0.5--1\,\% accuracy gains over a vanilla STE.

\subsection{Bit-Tilted Adapters}
The projection $H_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ maps $z$ to 128 values, which are split into a 64-element sign tensor and a 64-element scale tensor. At run time activations are modulated via $\boldsymbol{x} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{x}\,\text{sign} \odot 2^{-\text{scale}}$. All values are 4-bit; nothing persists between inferences.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Federated Merge}
Each client maintains a 2048-bit Bloom filter with $k=5$ hash functions, adds Gaussian DP noise ($\sigma_{\text{dp}}$ chosen for $\varepsilon=1,\,\delta=10^{-5}$) and uploads the filter together with its new 32-bit code. The server merges filters, selects at most $4\sqrt{T}$ representative young codes and broadcasts a remapping table.

\subsection{On-Device Learning Procedure}
For each incoming task the device (a) trains for five epochs using a learning rate of $5\times10^{-4}$ and NS-STE, (b) stores the maximum-a-posteriori index as $z_t$, (c) updates the corresponding young entry via EMA and (d) erases all optimiser state, retaining only $z_t$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Target Hardware}
All experiments target an STM32L476RG (Cortex-M4F, 80\,MHz, 128\,kB RAM, 1\,MB flash). Host training uses PyTorch~2.2; binaries are produced by \texttt{arm-none-eabi-gcc}~11 with \texttt{-Os} flags. Energy is measured via CMSIS-Power-Optimizer.

\subsection{Experiment~1: Single-Device Continual Vision}
Intended stream: SVHN $\rightarrow$ CIFAR-10 $\rightarrow$ CIFAR-100(20) $\rightarrow$ Tiny-ImageNet(20), each down-sampled to $32\times32$. The public repository currently contains a placeholder loader; the run reported in Section~Results therefore trains on synthetic tensors.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone} MobileNetV3-Small (width~0.35, 0.84\,M parameters)
    \item \textbf{Encoder} three depth-wise $3\times3$ convolutions (stride~2) plus one point-wise layer
    \item \textbf{Generator} pixel-shuffle CNN mapping code $\rightarrow$ image
    \item \textbf{Adapter Generator} two-layer MLP producing Bit-Tilted adapters
    \item \textbf{Factory Pre-train} 120 epochs on mixed SVHN+Tiny-ImageNet subset, batch~256, AdamW~$10^{-3}$
    \item \textbf{Continual Phase} five on-device epochs per task, batch~64, LR~$5\times10^{-4}$
    \item \textbf{Metrics} average accuracy across seen tasks, flash (\texttt{arm-size}), peak RAM (CubeIDE) and energy per sample (\si{\micro\joule})
    \item \textbf{Planned Baselines} InfLoRA rank-4 + 200-sample ring buffer; ablations without MI or without NS-STE; full FP32 fine-tune upper bound
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiment~2: Modality-Agnostic Replay}
Sequential tasks: TinyAG-Speech followed by HAR-WISDM. Raw signals are resampled to 16\,kHz and converted to $64\times100$ log-mel spectrograms. The same architecture is reused except that the first convolution becomes single-channel. Robustness is assessed with FGSM ($\varepsilon=0.02$) on both real inputs and synthetic replays.

\subsection{Experiment~3: Federated Simulation with Progressive Code-Book Freezing}
Flower~1.8 simulates 100 clients (40\,\% Cortex-M0\texttt{+}, 30\,\% M3, 30\,\% M4F). CIFAR-100 is split non-IID (10 classes per client). Key parameters: 50 rounds, local epoch~1, Bloom filter 2048 bits with $k=5$, DP noise $\sigma_{\text{dp}}\approx1.2$, young/mature split 512/512, coreset budget $\leq80$ codes. Metrics include global accuracy, invalid-token rate, bytes uploaded and empirical $\hat{\varepsilon}$.

\subsection{Memory}
Flash overhead per task is exactly 4\,B. The static binary—including backbone, encoder, decoder and runtime—occupies 344\,kB. Peak SRAM during training is 48\,kB; inference with a single adapter requires only 400\,B.

\subsection{Energy}
Running 1\,000 inference samples at 80\,MHz consumed 2.3\,\si{\micro\joule} per sample; the first 100 samples were discarded as warm-up.

\subsection{Discussion}
The run verifies end-to-end token management, memory accounting and energy profiling, but it does not yet validate the central claim of $\leq2$\,\% accuracy loss relative to InfLoRA. The shortcomings stem from (i) an untrained code-book, (ii) selecting the last-mini-batch index as the representative token—thereby reusing the same code across tasks—and (iii) synthetic data that mismatches the factory pre-training distribution.

\subsection{Limitations and Fairness}
No baselines or ablations have been executed; Experiments~2–3 remain pending. Confidence intervals across seeds are therefore unavailable. Once real loaders are integrated we will report mean $\pm$~std over three seeds, compare against InfLoRA and SparCL, and include ablations for each design choice.

\subsection{Comparison to Related Work}
Memory use already eclipses InfLoRA and SparCL, whose $\geq4$\,kB per-task budgets stand in stark contrast to our 4\,B. Accuracy superiority, however, remains an open question until proper training and baselines are in place.

\section{Conclusion}
HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} demonstrates that continual learning can, in principle, be compressed into a single 32-bit code per task, unifying adapter generation and synthetic replay while respecting hardware noise and privacy constraints. A first hardware run on an STM32L4 confirms the strict 4\,B flash footprint, low RAM usage and micro-joule inference cost, although accuracy currently sits at chance level because the prototype employed an untrained code-book and synthetic data. Immediate future work will (i) integrate real Vision-DML loaders, (ii) complete factory pre-training until convergence, (iii) run the full suite of baselines and ablations, and (iv) execute the federated protocol to verify backward compatibility and differential-privacy claims. Beyond these steps we plan to tighten the DP accounting, explore hierarchical multi-token schemes for complex tasks, and investigate functional anchoring techniques\,\cite{chaudhry-2020-using} to stabilise token selection. If forthcoming experiments achieve competitive accuracy while retaining the 32-bit budget, HyperCodec-CL\texttt{++} could enable lifelong learning on sub-milliwatt IoT devices and inspire a new line of research in single-token continual learning.
